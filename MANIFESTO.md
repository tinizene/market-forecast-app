# Get Rich Slow

### A manifesto for shrewd investing in underserved markets

---

Somewhere right now, someone with an extra $150 this month is deciding what to do
with it. A sports bet with long odds. A lottery ticket. A "guaranteed profit" signal
group charging a subscription fee to tell them when to buy and sell a currency pair.
A pyramid scheme dressed up as a cooperative. Not because they're reckless — because
these are the loudest, most accessible options in front of them, and nobody ever sat
them down and explained the alternative in language that assumed they were smart but
simply new to this.

We think that's a solvable problem. This is what we believe.

## The house always wins — except when you own it

A bet is a transfer of money from the many to the few, by design. The odds are set so
that, on average, across enough tries, the house wins and the player loses. It doesn't
matter how careful or clever you are; the mathematics of the game is against you from
the start.

Owning a small slice of real, productive businesses is a different kind of
participation entirely. When you buy an index fund, you're not betting against a
house — you're becoming a tiny part-owner of hundreds of real companies that make
things, employ people, and — over long stretches of history — have tended to grow in
value as a group, even though any single year, or any single company, can lose money.
This is not a guarantee. Markets fall, sometimes hard, sometimes for years at a
stretch. But it is a fundamentally different mathematical proposition than a bet
against a bookmaker, and that difference is the whole foundation of what we do.

## Slow is not a consolation prize

"Get rich slow" is not a euphemism for "settle for less." It's a description of how
wealth has actually, historically, been built by ordinary people with ordinary
incomes: small amounts, invested consistently, left alone for a long time, compounding
quietly in the background while life happens around them. Ten years. Twenty years. Not
because patience is a virtue we're asking you to perform, but because it is the
single most reliable edge an investor with $100 or $200 a month actually has. You
cannot out-trade a hedge fund. You can out-wait one.

Every feature we build asks one question: does this help someone make a better
decision over the next twenty years, or does it just make today more exciting? We
build for the first answer, every time — even when the second one would get more
clicks.

## Shrewd, not clever

Shrewdness, to us, looks unglamorous: understanding what an index actually is before
investing in one. Comparing two nearly-identical funds and choosing the one with a
lower fee, because a fraction of a percent compounds into real money over decades.
Preferring a basket of hundreds of companies over a bet on one. Understanding that a
dividend is a real cash payment, not a promise, and that it can be reduced or stopped.
Contributing the same amount on a schedule, regardless of what the news says this
week, because trying to time the market has a worse long-run track record than not
trying at all.

None of this is exciting. All of it works better, over time, than the alternative
most people are offered instead.

## What we won't do

We won't tell you a fund is a good idea because someone paid us to say so. We won't
manufacture a confidence score to make a recommendation feel more certain than the
underlying data supports. We won't put a specific fund, stock, or "opportunity" in
front of you because a company sponsored the placement rather than because the
numbers earned it. If we ever accept advertising or sponsorship to keep this free, it
will be for things that don't sit inside the recommendation itself — never a
pay-to-play seat at the table of what we tell you to consider. The moment that line
moves, everything else in this document stops meaning anything, and we know it.

## Who this is for

This is for the investor nobody built a product for: the person with $100 or $200 a
month, not $10,000; the person whose nearest brokerage is an app on their phone, not a
building downtown; the person who has been told, implicitly or explicitly, that
investing is for other people, in other countries, with more money. It isn't. The
mathematics of a diversified index fund doesn't check your postal code. The tools have
simply never been translated into language, and access, that assumed you belonged
there.

## Start slow. Start small. Start now.

You don't need $10,000 to start. You don't need to predict the next crash or the next
boom. You need a small amount, a long timeline, and a clear-eyed understanding of what
you're actually doing — which is exactly what the rest of this app is for. Start with
[Learn](./learn.html).
