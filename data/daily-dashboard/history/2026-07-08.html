1. Market Regime & Executive Summary

Regime classification: Event-Driven, led by an unscheduled geopolitical/energy shock rather than a single central-bank storyline, even as two separate central-bank threads run in parallel. VIX remains surprisingly calm at 15.88 (+0.44%, still risk-on) and US cash equities are actually firmer today (S&P 500 +0.66% to 7,532.82, Nasdaq +1.22% to 26,148.20), even though oil has spiked over 5% and the US 10Y sits at a two-week high of 4.50% — a market compartmentalizing a real commodity/geopolitical shock rather than showing broad-based fear. Tuesday's session showed more overt stress (chip-stock-led equity pullback, Nikkei 225 -1.75% to ~68,520), but today's US tape has stabilized.

The single biggest cross-asset driver today is the escalating Iran/Strait of Hormuz crisis: Iran's Revolutionary Guard struck a third commercial vessel transiting the Strait in 24 hours (following missile strikes on a Qatari LNG carrier and a Saudi-flagged tanker on Tuesday), prompting the US to conduct retaliatory strikes on more than 80 Iranian targets and the US Treasury to revoke the waiver that had allowed Iran to sell oil — together threatening to unravel a Strait-reopening ceasefire reached less than three weeks ago. Oil has jumped in two stages (Brent settled +3% at $74.16 after the tanker attacks, then extended above $76, +5.6%, after the Treasury move) while gold has fallen (~$4,122/oz, a "safe-haven paradox" as oil-driven inflation fears and hawkish-Fed positioning outweigh traditional safe-haven demand). Layered underneath: the RBNZ (Reserve Bank of New Zealand, New Zealand) delivered its first rate hike in three years (25bp to 2.50%) today, lifting NZD broadly, while the Fed's (US Federal Reserve's) June meeting minutes are due later today (2:00pm ET) carrying unusual weight since Chair Kevin Warsh took the unprecedented step of withholding his own dot-plot projection in June. USD/JPY continues to trade near its weakest yen level in roughly four decades even through this crisis — notably, yen has not caught the safe-haven bid a geopolitical shock would typically produce.
