1. Market Regime & Executive Summary

Regime classification: Event-Driven, dual-track — a genuine "good news on inflation, bad news on geopolitics" session, with US June CPI cooling sharply even as the Iran/Strait of Hormuz crisis escalated to its most severe point yet. US equities rose (S&P 500 +0.38% to 7,543.59, Nasdaq +0.9% to 26,107.01 on semiconductor strength, Dow roughly flat +0.02% to 52,508.27) and the US 10Y yield fell to 4.583% on a much cooler-than-expected June CPI print — yet VIX rose 14.2% to 17.16 anyway, an unusual divergence reflecting the Iran/Hormuz war-risk premium building underneath a rates-driven equity rally. Oil extended its rally sharply further (Brent +3.7% to +4.5% depending on source, into the mid-$80s), the highest level of this entire crisis.

Today was dominated by two simultaneous, partly offsetting stories. First, US June CPI surprised sharply to the downside: headline CPI fell 0.4% m/m (vs. -0.2% expected), pulling the annual rate to 3.5% (vs. 3.8% expected, down from May's 4.2%); core CPI was flat m/m (vs. +0.2% expected) at 2.6% y/y (vs. 2.9% expected) — a genuine, broad-based cooling led by a 5.7% m/m energy drop (largest since April 2020) and flat services-ex-energy costs. However, new Fed Chair Kevin Warsh's first congressional testimony (House Financial Services Committee) pushed back hard against a dovish read, stating the Committee has "no tolerance for persistently elevated inflation" and that today's data is not "mission accomplished" — keeping the September-hike debate alive. Q2 bank earnings from JPMorgan (EPS $6.14 vs. $5.85 expected) and Wells Fargo (EPS $2.00 vs. $1.72 expected) added a positive equity undertone. Second, the Iran/Hormuz crisis escalated further: the US reimposed a full naval blockade of Iranian ports (effective 20:00 GMT) alongside fresh strikes near Sirik and Bandar Abbas, while Iran retaliated with strikes on shipping in Omani waters and attacks on Kuwait, Bahrain, and Jordan — Kuwait's navy intercepted a ballistic missile, five cruise missiles, and 33 drones in a single wave. Trump separately scrapped his proposed 20% Hormuz transit toll, replacing it with bilateral trade deals with Gulf allies. USD/JPY eased modestly to ~162.20 on the broad-dollar pullback, still without any distinct safe-haven bid despite the crisis's worst day yet.
