1. Market Regime & Executive Summary

Regime classification: Geopolitical Risk-On Overlaid on Hawkish Monetary Policy & Earnings Disappointment — A Fragile Equilibrium Between Inflation Concerns (Fed hawkish) and Supply Disruption Risk (Iran escalation).

On the evening of July 28 (Asia time) through early morning July 29 (US time), Iran launched ballistic missile attacks on US military positions, breaking the June 2026 ceasefire agreement. The US and Saudi Arabia responded with retaliatory strikes on "terrorist logistics and weapons sites" in eastern Iraq. This geopolitical escalation has immediately triggered a sharp rally in oil futures: Brent September futures jumped 3.42% to $86.97, WTI up 3.58% to $82.09.

The Fed held rates as expected at 3.50%-3.75%, but Chair Kevin Warsh (known as an inflation hawk) emphasized that inflation remains above target and price stability must be preserved. There was no explicit dovish commentary on equity weakness.

Bottom line: This report publishes a PARTIAL RECOVERY from July 28's capitulation. The critical catalyst is Iran's overnight escalation, which breaks the "pure earnings-disappointment = demand destruction" thesis and reintroduces supply-risk premium into oil and commodities. Confidence rises from 32/100 to 45/100.

Three Themes Driving Everything Right Now

  • Geopolitical escalation is back; supply risk has returned. Iran's ballistic missile attack and the US/Saudi retaliation break the June 2026 ceasefire and reintroduce Strait of Hormuz risk. Oil has rallied +3%. If Hormuz remains disrupted, oil could spike to $100+, highly supportive for commodity currencies.
  • The Fed remains hawkish, but oil support is offsetting equities weakness. Warsh's hold with inflation-focused language means no near-term rate cut. However, oil strength from geopolitical risk is providing a floor for commodity currencies.
  • Central banks are fragmented on response. The Fed (Warsh) is staying hawkish, BoE is expected cautious, BoJ is expected hawkish language. China's PBoC may cut. No consensus = elevated volatility.

11. Historical Parallel Watch

The current regime parallels the April 2022 "oil shock recovery" when Russia's invasion of Ukraine triggered a 40% oil spike over two weeks. In that parallel, oil support eventually exhausted (by late April 2022) as demand destruction reasserted, and risk assets re-weakened.

However, the April 2022 parallel also showed that oil shocks can create profitable 2-3 week trading windows for commodity FX and carry recovery before the narrative shifts. If today's Iran escalation plays out similarly, NZD/JPY and AUD recovery opportunities should emerge by August 1-5.

If Hormuz closes or escalation deepens, the parallel breaks down and we enter a true geopolitical crisis (1990-1991 Gulf War parallel, which saw a months-long oil rally). The April 2022 parallel is the base case; the 1990 parallel is the tail risk.
