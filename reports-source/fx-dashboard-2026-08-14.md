The catalyst was **NOT** a Hormuz resolution (which would have cratered oil)—rather, the deal timeline stretched far longer than anticipated, introducing a new risk: **demand destruction.** The IEA cut its global oil demand outlook, warning that prolonged conflict and elevated prices are weighing on consumption, and OPEC lowered its 2026 demand growth forecast to 580,000 bpd, marking its fourth consecutive downward revision. On the negotiation front, Iran's FM said the two countries are "very close" to a Hormuz deal but warned reopening is "subject to other conditions," including U.S. compensation, and Trump told Axios the U.S. is "only semi-negotiating," relying on the naval blockade for pressure. This **extended the timeline** (removing the "quick spike to $100" catalyst) while leaving the supply floor intact but capped by demand weakness.

**Match or divergence — and why:**  
**Divergence.** The macro thesis—that Hormuz supply disruption would support oil—was **directionally correct** (Brent did NOT collapse; it held $87–89, well above pre-conflict levels), but the **timing and path assumptions failed.** Specifically:  
1. **Wrong catalyst sequencing:** We modeled two outcomes—(a) quick deal → spike as shorts cover, or (b) no deal → sustained rally on supply scarcity. What actually happened was (c) **slow-motion deal with extended timeline,** which kept the floor (no collapse) but introduced demand destruction that capped upside. The IEA/OPEC demand downgrades were not in our original model as a primary risk.  
2. **Positioning reversal:** We assumed energy positioning was under-owned and ready to chase a breakout. Instead, oil prices sold off more than 7% last week after Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent told CNBC a deal to open Hormuz could come soon—indicating that length had already rebuilt in anticipation of a deal, and when the deal stalled, longs were forced out rather than fresh shorts entering.  
3. **Technical breakdown:** The $87.75 stop was set just below the July–August consolidation range. It was hit not on a fundamental "all-clear" headline but on **technical momentum failure**—the base at $89 broke on heavy volume as the demand-downgrade headlines compounded the deal-delay uncertainty.

The root cause: we overweighted the supply-shock catalyst and underweighted the demand-response (elastic at $90+ for an extended period, destroying growth forecasts).
