The market repriced from "Hormuz fully closed" to "Hormuz partially open with workarounds," and oil fell from the entry zone to ~$88, hitting the $87.75 stop.

Match or divergence — and why: Divergence. The thesis was directionally correct about geopolitical risk, but wrong about the market's pricing of that risk. Specifically, we modeled a binary "closed vs. open" scenario, but reality was a spectrum: partial closures, covert tanker flows, and faster-than-expected diplomatic progress (Iran-Oman talks) reduced the physical supply impact from our assumed 20–30% to an actual ~10–15%. The root cause was overestimating the rigidity of the supply disruption and underestimating the speed at which markets would discount partial solutions. Additionally, the US EIA forecast now expects Brent to average around $85 per barrel in Q3 2026, far below our $102–108 target, confirming that the consensus view shifted materially lower after our entry.

Additional risk-limiting techniques relevant to trading generally include:

  1. Position sizing: Size each trade so no single idea risks more than a small, fixed percentage of total account equity (commonly cited as 1–2%). On a $50,000 account with a 1% risk rule, this $90.50 → $87.75 stop (–$2.75 risk per barrel) would dictate a position size of ($50,000 × 0.01) / $2.75 ≈ 182 barrels, keeping total risk at $500 regardless of the idea's outcome.
  2. Trailing stops: As a trade moves favorably, trail the stop to breakeven or beyond to lock in partial gains. (This idea never moved favorably, so trailing was not applicable.)
  3. Scaling confidence to size: Reduce position size on lower-confidence ideas. This idea was published at 73/100 — respectable but not the tracker's highest conviction — so a trader might have sized it at 0.75% risk instead of 1%, further limiting the dollar loss.
  4. Diversification: Avoid concentrating risk in correlated ideas. A portfolio holding both Long Brent and Short AUD/USD (both invalidated) would have suffered compounding losses; diversifying across uncorrelated pairs (e.g., long EUR/CHF, which played out) smooths the equity curve.
