Bottom Line: Markets are in a pre-Jackson Hole limbo. Oil's retreat, sticky inflation, and Warsh's unknown tone make directional conviction low. The bar for a new high-conviction trade idea remains high—wait for Friday's speech to clarify whether the Fed is paused or finished.

Then (Aug 2019):

  • Fed had cut 25 bp in July 2019 (first cut since 2008).
  • Markets were pricing 50–75 bp of additional cuts by year-end 2019.
  • Jackson Hole speech (Aug 23, 2019): Powell described July's cut as a "mid-cycle adjustment," not the start of a full easing cycle.
  • Market reaction: USD rallied, equities sold off (S&P 500 –2.6% in following week), bond yields rose (10-year +15 bp).
  • Lesson: Markets had front-run aggressive easing; Powell's pushback caused a sharp reversal.

Now (Aug 2026):

  • Fed has held at 3.50–3.75% since prior meetings.
  • Markets pricing ~33% Sept hike odds, but EUR/USD rallied 1.15 → 1.1656 (pricing in "Fed is done" narrative).

  • Potential market reaction: If Warsh hawkish, USD rallies (EUR/USD 1.1656 → 1.1500, USD/JPY 158 → 162), equities sell off, AUD/JPY (our open idea) vulnerable.

Key Difference: In 2019, markets were pricing cuts; Powell pushed back by saying "one and done." In 2026, markets are pricing pause; Warsh could push back by saying "pause but not done, hike still live if inflation doesn't cooperate." The directional impact is the same: dollar strength, risk-off.

Why This Parallel Matters:

  • It shows that Jackson Hole speeches can materially shift market pricing, especially when the new Fed Chair (Powell in 2019 was still establishing his credibility post-Yellen; Warsh in 2026 is brand-new post-Powell) uses the platform to set expectations.
  • It warns against front-running consensus. In 2019, the consensus "Fed is cutting aggressively" was wrong. In 2026, the consensus "Fed is done hiking" could be wrong if Warsh pushes back.

Contrarian Lesson for Today: If you believe Warsh will deliver a "dovish" speech (Fed patient, inflation moderating), you're betting on the consensus. If you believe he'll deliver a "hawkish" speech (inflation still a problem, Sept hike live), you're betting on a 2019-style reversal. The trade is to wait for the speech rather than picking a side in advance, because the 2019 parallel shows the speech itself is the catalyst, not the pre-speech positioning.
