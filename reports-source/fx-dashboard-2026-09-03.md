**Historical parallel #1: October 1973 – January 1974 (Yom Kippur War / First Oil Shock)**

**What happened:**
- October 1973: Yom Kippur War (Israel vs Egypt/Syria) triggers an OPEC oil embargo. Oil prices quadruple from ~$3/bbl to $12/bbl by March 1974.
- Central banks: US Fed under Arthur Burns was already tightening (rates had risen from 3% in 1971 to 10% by mid-1973 to fight inflation). The oil shock re-accelerated inflation (US CPI went from 3% in 1972 to 12% by 1974). The Fed kept tightening into the recession (rates peaked at 13% in 1974), causing the 1973–75 recession (GDP -0.5% in 1974, -0.2% in 1975).
- FX impact: USD was weak in 1973 (post-Bretton Woods collapse in 1971, dollar devalued), but the Fed's aggressive tightening into 1974 reversed that, and USD rallied vs DEM, JPY, GBP in 1974–75.
- Equities: S&P 500 peaked in January 1973 at 120, crashed to 62 by October 1974 (-48%), one of the worst bear markets in history.

**Similarity to today:**
- Oil shock driven by Middle East conflict ✅
- Central banks tightening into an oil-driven inflation spike ✅
- Stagflation risk (growth slowing, inflation rising) ✅

**Difference from today:**
- 1973 oil shock was an *embargo* (supply deliberately cut), whereas 2026 is a *disruption* (conflict threatens supply, but shipments still flowing at 40% of normal per index 46-6).
- 1973 Fed was already at 10% rates and went to 13% (massive tightening from a high base). 2026 Fed is at 3.50–3.75% and may go to 4.00% (modest tightening from a low base).
- 1973 had no coordinated global central bank response. 2026 has Fed, ECB, BoJ all moving in the same direction (tightening), which is historically rare.

**Outcome in 1973–74:** USD rallied (Fed out-hawked other CBs), equities crashed, recession followed. If 2026 rhymes, we'd expect USD strength, equity weakness, and FX vol to spike (carry trades unwind, safe havens bid).

**Probability this parallel plays out:** Moderate (40%). The oil shock is real, but it's not as severe as 1973 (oil at $95 vs $12 in 1974 dollars ≈ $80 in 2024 dollars, so similar magnitude, but no embargo). Central banks are more coordinated (all tightening together), which could prevent the "one CB out-hawks the rest" dynamic that drove USD strength in 1974.
