Regime classification: Event-Driven, deepening — the Iran/Strait of Hormuz crisis has escalated from "threatened ceasefire" to an explicitly declared ceasefire collapse, while yesterday's live FOMC-minutes wildcard has now resolved (hawkish), removing one source of uncertainty even as it reinforces the broad-dollar backdrop. The single biggest cross-asset driver remains the Iran/Strait of Hormuz crisis, which took a sharp turn Wednesday when President Trump, speaking at the NATO summit in Ankara, declared the three-week-old ceasefire "over" and warned Iran "won't have a country" if it closes the Strait — a marked escalation from Tuesday's framing of a ceasefire merely "threatened." US forces conducted a second consecutive day of strikes on Iranian targets, and roughly 6,000 seafarers remain stranded in the Persian Gulf. Separately, the Fed's (US Federal Reserve's) June meeting minutes, released Wednesday at 2:00pm ET, resolved more hawkish than expected — the Committee removed earlier hints that rate cuts could come soon and shifted its emphasis toward bringing inflation back under control, reinforcing dollar strength on top of the crisis-driven safe-haven bid. Adding a third thread, China's June CPI printed a soft +1.0% y/y (below the 1.1% consensus, down from May's 1.2%), a mild risk-negative data point for AUD and broader risk sentiment. USD/JPY continues to trade near its weakest yen level in roughly four decades — remarkably, yen still has not caught a safe-haven bid even now that the ceasefire has been explicitly declared over, arguably this week's single most notable cross-asset anomaly.
