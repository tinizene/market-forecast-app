1. Market Regime & Executive Summary

Regime classification: Event-Driven, tilting Risk-Off — the Iran/Strait of Hormuz crisis escalated sharply over the weekend from a declared-over ceasefire to a formal Iranian closure of the Strait and a US naval blockade, and today's market reaction is more clearly risk-off than any prior session in this crisis. VIX jumped intraday to ~16.39 (+9.05% vs Friday's ~15.03 close), and US cash equities fell broadly (Dow -0.2%, S&P 500 -0.7%, Nasdaq -1.4% on renewed AI-stock pressure) rather than shrugging off the news as in prior sessions. The US 10Y yield holds at 4.58% (little changed from Thursday's 4.59%), and oil extended its rally sharply (Brent surging as much as 4.2-7.1% on different measures to the $79-81 area).

The single biggest cross-asset driver is a major weekend escalation in the Iran/Strait of Hormuz crisis: Iran formally announced it had closed the Strait of Hormuz "until further notice," President Trump responded by announcing the US would reinstate a naval blockade of Iranian ships and begin charging a 20% toll on all cargo transiting the waterway, and Iran retaliated with strikes against US allies Kuwait, Jordan, and Qatar — the fourth US strike on Iran in a week followed an Iranian attack on a Cyprus-flagged container ship. Shipping through the Strait has fallen roughly 52% week-on-week. Oil has surged further (Brent briefly topping $81, WTI in the $72-75 range), while gold has continued to fall (~$4,064-4,107/oz) in the same "safe-haven paradox" this crisis has produced since last week. Markets now turn to Tuesday's US June CPI print and a slate of major bank earnings (JPMorgan, Goldman Sachs, Wells Fargo) — the Fed's (US Federal Reserve's) next real data test after last Wednesday's hawkish June minutes. USD/JPY remains near its weakest yen level in roughly four decades, still without any safe-haven bid despite the worst escalation of this crisis yet.
