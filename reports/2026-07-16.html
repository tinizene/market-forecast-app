1. Market Regime & Executive Summary

Regime: AI-Valuation Risk-Off Meets a Widening Gulf Shutdown — two distinct shocks arriving together, each strong enough to move markets on its own.

Asian equities sold off sharply on a semiconductor-led AI-valuation unwind: Japan's Nikkei 225 fell 2.6% to below 67,000 (Topix -0.8% to 4,055), snapping a two-day winning streak as AI-linked names (Advantest, SoftBank Group, Tokyo Electron) tumbled. South Korea's KOSPI plunged 7%+ (Kosdaq -5%). Australia's ASX 200 (+0.23%), Hong Kong's Hang Seng (+1.70%) and mainland China's Shanghai Composite (-0.22%) were comparatively unbothered — a mixed regional picture, not a uniform flight from risk.

Separately, the Iran/Strait of Hormuz crisis (US vs. Iran) widened further: the Strait is effectively closed to commercial shipping (~10 vessels transited vs. a normal ~88/day), the US completed a second wave of strikes Wednesday, a naval blockade of Iranian ports is reimposed, and ~6,000 seafarers remain stranded. Brent extended its rally to a fourth straight session (~$85.90 vs. Wednesday's $84.73 close); gold held broadly steady (~$4,060-4,070/oz) — still no strong safe-haven bid, a paradox flagged repeatedly in this report.

Fed Chair Kevin Warsh's first Senate Banking Committee testimony (US Federal Reserve, United States) reaffirmed anti-inflation rhetoric but gave few fresh clues on the rate path, and faced sharp ethics questioning from Sen. Warren — a non-event for the Fed-vs-market-pricing tension flagged yesterday. The Bank of Canada (Canada) delivered a fully-priced 6th straight hold at 2.25%.

Executive takeaway: this is two shocks on separate clocks — a contained semiconductor-valuation correction, and a still-escalating Gulf war whose main transmission channel remains oil, not broad risk-off (yet).
